DISEASE DESCRIPTION

Early blight is a common fungal disease affecting tomato plants. It commonly produces dark lesions on older leaves and may lead to yellowing, leaf drop and reduced plant vigor when the infection progresses.

Primary Cause: Fungal infection
Pathogen: Alternaria solani
Favorable Conditions: High humidity & warm temperature
Transmission: Wind and water splash

OBSERVED / ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS

✓Dark circular lesions with characteristic concentric target rings
✓Brown necrotic spots on lower mature foliage
✓Yellowing (chlorosis) halo surrounding infected spots
✓Progressive leaf curling, drying, and premature leaf drop
✓Reduced leaf canopy health and sunscald vulnerability
* Symptoms shown above represent visual characteristics associated with the predicted disease class. They should not be interpreted as laboratory confirmation.

CAUSE & RISK FACTORS

Primary Cause: Fungal infection (Alternaria solani)
Environmental & Field Risk Factors:
  • High ambient relative humidity (>80%)
  • Poor air circulation within dense canopy
  • Wet foliage due to overhead sprinkler irrigation
  • Infected crop debris left on soil surface
  • Warm environmental temperatures (24-29°C)

RECOMMENDED CROP CARE PLAN

Immediate Actions:
  1. 1. Remove severely infected leaves and dispose of them safely (do not compost).
  2. 2. Maintain proper spacing between tomato plants (24-36 inches).
  3. 3. Avoid unnecessary overhead irrigation; use drip lines at soil level.
  4. 4. Improve canopy air circulation by pruning lower sucker leaves.
  5. 5. Clear infected plant debris around the base of the crop.
  6. 6. Monitor adjacent plants daily for early spot formation.
Preventive Actions:
  • • Use certified disease-free healthy planting seed material.
  • • Maintain strict field sanitation and tool disinfection.
  • • Avoid excessive moisture accumulation on leaf blades.
  • • Monitor crops regularly during warm, humid weather cycles.
  • • Follow locally recommended crop rotation (3-year interval).
